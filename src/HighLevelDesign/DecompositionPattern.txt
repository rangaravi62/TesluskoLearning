===========================================================
Decomposition Pattern
===========================================================

Decomposition Pattern is one of the first patterns to understand in microservices

Decomposition pattern is used to decide how a monolithic system should be divided into microservices.
Two common approaches are decomposition by business capability and decomposition by subdomain using Domain-Driven Design.
The goal is to create loosely coupled services where each service owns a specific business responsibility.


1. Decompose by Business Capability --->

Split the application based on business capabilities

Suppose we have an e-commerce monolith:

E-Commerce Application
        |
        ├── User Management
        ├── Product Management
        ├── Order Management
        ├── Payment
        ├── Inventory
        └── Shipping

We can decompose it into:

                    API Gateway
                         |
       -------------------------------------
       |        |        |       |         |
     User    Product   Order   Payment   Shipping
    Service  Service  Service  Service   Service
                         |
                    Inventory
                     Service

One microservice should own one clearly defined business responsibility.



2. Decompose by Subdomain --->

Instead of simply looking at business functions, we identify different domains/subdomains within the business.

Decomposition by subdomain means identifying different business areas in the application and creating microservices around
those areas. Each subdomain owns its own business logic and data.

smaller business area inside a bigger business domain.

Ex:

E-Commerce
   |
   ├── Orders
   ├── Payments
   ├── Inventory
   ├── Shipping
   └── Customers

These are subdomains

Then we can create microservices around those subdomains:

Order Subdomain      → Order Service
Payment Subdomain    → Payment Service
Inventory Subdomain  → Inventory Service
Shipping Subdomain   → Shipping Service

The key idea is that each subdomain has its own business rules.
For example, the Payment subdomain may contain rules like:

Process payment
Refund payment
Check payment status
Validate payment method
